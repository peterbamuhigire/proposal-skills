Status: operational reference, 2026-08-27

## Source limits and usable lessons

Three supplied files—Bolitho, Mann, and *Natural English Reading & Writing Skills*—are title-only stubs (111–923 bytes), so no framework is attributed to them. David’s ceramic manual supports observation, controlled fields, classification, glossary discipline, and traceable interpretation; it is not a general prose guide. Gupta’s 2019 vocabulary book supports selective word choice and confusable-word checks, not ornamental diction. Pinnacle’s error-practice book supports checks for agreement, articles, tense, prepositions, pronouns, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling, and cohesion, while exam keys may simplify usage. Betsis and Mamas’s CAE models support task coverage, audience/register, genre, paragraphing, balanced judgement, and recommendation; model openings are not proposal boilerplate.

## Proposal standard

Write for the evaluator’s actual decision. Human proposal writing is tailored, evidence-bearing, and candid about delivery conditions. It makes the proposer sound like a capable person who has understood the assignment, not like a machine filling headings.

### Five passes

1. Name evaluator, procurement setting, purpose, decision, and mandatory constraints.
2. Build the evaluator journey: need; understanding; method; deliverables; team; schedule; risk; proof; value; next step.
3. Map every material claim to proposer evidence, ToR language, or verified external evidence. Label assumptions and gaps.
4. Revise for direct sentences, exact verbs, varied rhythm, parallel lists, correct grammar, and clear links between activities and outputs.
5. Proof compliance, names, dates, prices, cross-references, forms, headings, and envelope separation; read aloud and run the proposal-specific gates.

## Craft rules

- British English and East African professional register are the default unless the buyer specifies otherwise.
- State the evaluator-relevant point early. Replace prestige adjectives with scope, proof, method, measure, owner, or boundary.
- Use rare vocabulary only when the sense, collocation, and register are exact.
- Check agreement, articles, tense/aspect, pronouns, relative clauses, prepositions, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling, and sentence boundaries.
- Give each paragraph a point, support, and implication. Do not enforce a fixed sentence count.
- For major promises, privately test **claim → evidence → warrant → countercase → implication**.
- Use transitions to show logic, not to decorate every paragraph. Vary rhythm because the thought varies.

## Anti-slop questions

Could another consultant submit this paragraph unchanged? Add the ToR’s actual language, the client’s context, the delivery mechanism, named proof, or a real constraint—or cut it. Does the method explain who does what, when, with what evidence? Do not use fake warmth, inflated capability, stock conclusions, or invented credentials. Do not add mistakes to imitate a human writer.

## Evidence record

Record buyer/purpose, ToR and profile, claim-proof map, terms checked, revision and proof status, unresolved gaps, reviewer, and date. Editorial polish never substitutes for compliance or evidence.
