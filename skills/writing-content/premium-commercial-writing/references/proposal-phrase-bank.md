# Proposal Phrase Bank: Section-by-Section Language Reference

Parent skill: [Premium Commercial Writing](../SKILL.md). Method: [proposal power-paragraph method](proposal-power-paragraph-method.md).

**When to read:** while drafting or revising any proposal section, EOI, retainer proposal or covering letter, and whenever the anti-slop gate flags generic wording. Use the slot-templates as sentence skeletons, not as copy. Fill every slot with a sourced fact; if a slot cannot be filled truthfully, delete the sentence rather than leave a vague one.

This bank is the engine's human-professional language standard. It is written for East African and international evaluators: measured, courteous, specific and evidence-led. The sentence-job structure is adapted from Debelak, D. (2006) *Perfect Phrases for Business Proposals and Business Plans*, McGraw-Hill; closing and objection phrasing draws on Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press; negotiation phrasing on Marcos, J., Guesalaga, R., Hough, A. and Vincent, R. (c. 2025) *The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page. The firm-profile differentiation test (points of difference versus points of parity) draws on Brown, R. (2016) *Build Your Reputation*, Capstone/Wiley, and the preference for process guarantees on Serling, B. (ed.) (2002) *How to Write Million Dollar Ads, Sales Letters and Web Marketing Pieces*, The Internet Marketing Center. All templates below are original to this engine. Example organisations are bracketed placeholders and all figures are illustrative; neither may be reused as fact.

**Slot notation:** `[Client]` `[Firm]` `[n]` `[%]` `[UGX amount]` `[date]` `[timeframe]` `[place]` `[named alternative]` `[metric]` `[baseline]` `[target]` `[role]` `[source]`.

**Universal rules**

- A named party is the subject: "[Client] is considering…", "[Firm] will…".
- A number, place or date appears in the first or second clause, and every number has a source.
- One idea per sentence; about 25 words or fewer.
- Customisation is shown in nouns (their sites, staff, members, districts), never claimed with adjectives.
- Conditional mood for benefits we cannot guarantee; indicative for facts.
- Price is stated plainly, first, and next to value. Never apologise for it.

## 1. Purpose line (first sentence of a letter proposal or executive summary)

**Job:** say what is proposed, for whom and to what end, in one sentence a reader could repeat.

**Keywords:** proposes to, will deliver, on behalf of, for [period], in response to [reference].

**Slot-templates**

1. This proposal sets out how [Firm] will help [Client] [outcome] by [date].
2. [Firm] proposes to [design / implement / review] [system] at [n] of [Client]'s [sites] over [n] months.
3. [Firm] submits this proposal in response to [tender reference / request dated …] for [service].
4. [Firm] proposes to take responsibility for [function] for [Client] from [date], in return for a [monthly / fixed] fee.
5. [Party A] and [Party B] propose a joint [programme] to [shared outcome] across [region].
6. The purpose of this proposal is to [secure / reduce / expand] [metric] for [Client] through [mechanism].

**Before → after**

- Before: "We are excited to present our comprehensive digital solution for your esteemed organisation."
- After: "This proposal sets out how [Firm] will rebuild [Client hardware wholesaler, Mukono]'s website and set up WhatsApp ordering for its three branches before the December trading peak."

## 2. Cover letter

**Job:** create trust and signal fit before the evaluator reads the body. Owned by [`01-cover-letter`](../../../pipeline/01-cover-letter/SKILL.md).

**Opening (client situation, not the firm)**

1. [Client]'s [programme / reform / expansion] depends on [capability] being in place by [date].
2. The Terms of Reference ask for [outcome]; the harder task is [overriding question in plain words].
3. Having studied [specific document or visit], we understand that [constraint] shapes every option.

**Why us (two or three proof points)**

4. [Firm] has delivered [comparable work] for [n] [client type] in [region], most recently [project] ([year]), where [result against baseline].
5. Our proposed team leader, [name], led [comparable assignment] and will be on the assignment for [%] of the contract period.
6. We are the [registered / accredited] [status] provider in [country], and our local team works in [languages].

**Compliance confirmation**

7. Our proposal responds to every requirement in the Terms of Reference; a compliance matrix is at Annex [x].
8. The offer remains valid for [n] days from the submission deadline. The financial proposal is sealed separately, as instructed.

**Value beyond the project (one sentence)**

9. Beyond [immediate result], the work would give [Client] [second-order capability], which positions it for [next opportunity].

**Close and next step**

10. We would welcome the opportunity to present our approach to the evaluation committee on a date that suits you.
11. [Name, role] is our point of contact at [phone] and [email] and can respond to clarification requests within [n] working hours.

**Tone:** one page; formal and courteous; "Yours faithfully" for an unnamed recipient and "Yours sincerely" for a named one; follow [east-african-english](../../../language/east-african-english/SKILL.md) for salutations and protocol.

**Before → after**

- Before: "We are pleased to submit our proposal and look forward to a mutually beneficial relationship."
- After: "The Ministry's plan to decentralise payroll approval to 14 districts depends on district officers trusting the new system in its first quarter. Our proposal is built around that trust test. We ran the same district roll-out for [comparable client] in 2025, where 92% of approvals moved online within ten weeks ([source])."

## 3. Executive summary

**Job:** make the award decision feel logical in two to three pages. Owned by [`02-executive-summary`](../../../pipeline/02-executive-summary/SKILL.md).

**Governing idea (one sentence)**

1. [Firm] proposes a [n]-phase [programme] that will [move metric from baseline to target] by [date], at a fixed fee of [amount] / within the budget ceiling.

**The client's problem (S1 → S2 → B)**

2. [Client] currently [situation], which costs [quantified consequence, sourced].
3. The Terms of Reference seek [desired state]; reaching it would [benefit].

**Pillars (three or four, one sentence each)**

4. **Method:** we will [approach], because [reason tied to the client's constraint].
5. **Proof:** we have done this for [n] comparable [clients], with [result] ([source]).
6. **Team:** [name] leads, supported by [specialists] who have [specific experience].
7. **Control:** [governance / QA / acceptance gate] protects [the outcome that matters most].

**Pre-answered concerns**

8. Evaluators usually ask three things before they commit: [concern 1], [concern 2], [concern 3]. Section [x] answers each directly.

**Benefits-forward close (during, at completion, beyond)**

9. During the assignment, [Client]'s staff will [learn / receive early findings].
10. At completion, [Client] will hold [deliverables] and [trained staff / working system].
11. Beyond the project, [metric] should [improve], giving [Client] [future opportunity].

**Before → after**

- Before: "Our experienced team will deliver innovative, high-quality results that exceed expectations."
- After: "[Client agro-input distributor] wants to cut invoice-to-payment time for its 1,200 stockists from 45 to 20 days by June. Faster collection matters because the company pre-finances each planting season. Mobile-money payment links on every invoice, with SMS reminders, cut collection times at two comparable distributors we support ([source])."

## 4. Understanding of the assignment: needs in the client's words

**Job:** show that we understand the situation as the client sees it, then stop the client choosing to do nothing. Owned by [`03-understanding-of-assignment`](../../../pipeline/03-understanding-of-assignment/SKILL.md).

**Hard rule:** at least one need is stated in the client's own words with its source, and at least one sentence states the cost of doing nothing.

**Perceived need (sentence 1)**

1. [Client] is considering [drastic option] because [pain, in the client's words].
2. In our meeting on [date], [role] described [problem] as "[their phrase]".
3. The Terms of Reference ([clause]) ask for [requirement]; behind that request is [underlying need].
4. [Client] wants [outcome] without giving up [constraint it will not trade].
5. [Client] currently [workaround], which [consequence].
6. [Client]'s [n] largest [customers / funders] now require [standard], and [team] needs help to [meet it].

**Cost of doing nothing (sentence 2; mandatory)**

7. If nothing changes, [Client] will continue to lose [amount / time / members] each [period] ([source]).
8. Each month of delay costs about [UGX amount] in [lost revenue / penalties / overtime], on [Client]'s own figures.
9. Without [change], [Client] risks [non-compliance with / exclusion from] [regulation or buyer requirement] from [date] ([source; mark VERIFY if regulatory]).
10. Patching [symptom] would keep [cost] in place; resolving [cause] would also [second benefit].
11. The alternatives under consideration would leave [Client] with [outdated / non-compliant] [asset] within [timeframe].

**Longer-term benefit (sentence 3 or 4)**

12. Resolving [need] now would position [Client] as [a dependable supplier / the preferred provider] to [audience].
13. [Outcome] would free [team] to focus on [higher-value work], which [Client] has said it wants.
14. It would also set the stage for [second phase / new market / next funding round].

**Joint cooperation or partnership variant**

15. Both [Party A] and [Party B] want to [expand into X], and neither has the [channel / capital / skills] to do it alone.

**Tone:** empathetic and precise. Mirror the client's language; do not diagnose it in our jargon. Name real local pressures (EFRIS e-invoicing, NSSF compliance, donor reporting formats, mobile-money reconciliation, power interruptions) and mark every regulatory statement for verification before submission.

**Before → after**

- Before: "Your organisation faces many challenges in today's competitive digital landscape."
- After: "[Client clinic, Kampala] is considering closing its weekend outpatient service, because bookings arrive by phone and, on the clinic's own register, 30 to 40% of patients do not attend. Every empty weekend slot is lost margin, since weekends carry the clinic's highest-value consultations. A reliable booking flow would let the clinic keep weekends open and build the patient loyalty it needs before opening a second site in Kira."

## 5. Project goals

**Job:** state the client's goal, show why it is the right goal, and show it is achievable.

1. [Client]'s goal is to [cut / raise] [metric] from [baseline] to [target] by [date].
2. The target matters because [reason the client chose it], which confirms [Client] is on a sound course.
3. [Client] can reach it by [mechanism 1] and [mechanism 2], both of which [Firm] has delivered before.
4. A preliminary review of [n] options suggests benefits of at least [n] times the cost ([method, source]).
5. The goal is ambitious. With [client contribution] alongside this proposal, it is achievable within [timeframe].
6. **If the goal cannot be met:** We have tested the target against [constraint]. At the stated budget and timetable, [target] is not achievable without [condition]; we propose [achievable variant] and explain the trade-off in Section [x].

**Before → after**

- Before: "Our goal is to deliver excellent results that exceed your expectations."
- After: "The Board's goal is to confirm 90% of online appointment requests within two working hours by April. Speed matters because patients who wait book a competing clinic the same day. Routing requests to a shared WhatsApp Business inbox with an automatic acknowledgement has met a similar target at [comparable client] ([source])."

## 6. Approach and methodology

**Job:** show judgement (options considered), fit (built for this client) and control (how the result is protected). Owned by [`06-methodology`](../../../pipeline/06-methodology/SKILL.md).

**Options considered (show judgement)**

1. We considered [n] ways to [reach the goal]: [option A], [option B] and [option C].
2. [Option A] is quickest to start but [drawback: cost / dependency / risk].
3. [Option B] costs least at the outset but would leave [Client] [exposed to / dependent on] [risk].
4. [Option C] takes [n] weeks longer but [decisive benefit tied to the client's stated goal].
5. We recommend [option C] because it best meets [the client's goal, in its words].
6. We ruled out [option] because [reason the client will accept].
7. If [budget / timing] outweighs [benefit], [option A] remains viable and we would price it separately.

**Built for this client (targeted message)**

8. Because [client-specific fact], the [design / sequence] in this proposal starts with [element]; that order is not our standard package.
9. [Firm] will assign [n] specialists to [Client]'s [premises / districts] for [n] weeks to [install / train / supervise].
10. The design draws on [Client]'s own [data / brand / constraint], not a template.
11. This structure lets [Client] test [idea] while risking only [small, bounded cost].

**Hypothesis-first and co-delivery**

12. We will work hypothesis-first: our starting hypothesis is [descriptive hypothesis]; in weeks 1 to 2 we test it against [data] and drop weak alternatives early.
13. Each workstream pairs one of our consultants with one of [Client]'s managers, so the knowledge stays with [Client] when we leave.
14. A series of [n] workshops moves from challenge, to choice, to operational commitments; each ends with named owners and dates.

**Control and acceptance**

15. [Deliverable] is accepted when [observable test] is met and signed off by [role].
16. Decisions on [scope / budget / brand] rest with [role]; [Firm] recommends and [Client] approves.
17. Issues not resolved at project level go to [named escalation] within [n] working days.

**Before → after**

- Before: "After careful analysis, we have determined the optimal solution."
- After: "We considered three routes to online ordering for [client bakery, Jinja]: a marketplace listing, a WhatsApp catalogue and an owned ordering site. The marketplace launches in a week but charges a commission on every order. A WhatsApp catalogue costs nothing but becomes hard to manage beyond roughly 40 orders a day. We recommend the owned site with a WhatsApp hand-off, because it protects margin at the 150-orders-a-day target the owners set."

## 7. Firm profile

**Job:** prove the firm is the right kind of firm for this work, not that it is large or old. Owned by [`04-firm-profile`](../../../pipeline/04-firm-profile/SKILL.md).

**Differentiation test before drafting:** for each claim ask "Could a competent competitor say this unchanged?" If yes, it is a point of parity (a table stake): keep it short or cut it. Lead with points of difference that are proved.

1. [Firm] is a [legal form] registered in [country] in [year], specialising in [narrow specialism] for [client type].
2. Since [year], [Firm] has delivered [n] [assignment type] in [countries], [n] of them for [client type].
3. [Firm] works only in [niche]; that focus is why we [capability competitors rarely have].
4. Our method, [named method], has been applied on [n] assignments and is documented in [publication / manual].
5. [Firm]'s [certification / registration] ([number], valid to [date]) covers [scope].
6. Our team of [n] includes [n] [specialists] based in [towns], working in [languages].

**Earned stature (only when true and evidenced)**

7. [Lead] writes [column / guidance] on [topic] for [publication] and has spoken on [topic] at [association event, year].
8. [Firm] contributed to [standard / guideline / sector report] published by [body] in [year].

**Before → after**

- Before: "We are a leading, innovative, full-service firm with a passionate team and extensive experience across many industries."
- After: "[Firm] builds booking and records systems for private health facilities in Uganda and Kenya. Since 2021 we have delivered 11 such systems, seven for facilities with more than 50 beds (list at Annex C). Our content lead spent six years in a hospital communications office, which is why our patient-facing copy is clinically reviewed before release."

## 8. Relevant experience and case studies

**Job:** prove transferability. Owned by [`05-relevant-experience`](../../../pipeline/05-relevant-experience/SKILL.md), which holds the case-study write-up structure.

1. **Stakes:** When [client type] in [place] faced [problem, with baseline], [consequence] was at risk.
2. **Options:** We weighed [n] responses: [option 1], [option 2] and [option 3].
3. **What we did:** We chose [option] because [reason], and [Firm]'s team [specific actions, first-hand].
4. **Result against baseline:** [Metric] moved from [baseline] to [result] within [timeframe] ([evidence source / client confirmation]).
5. **Role honesty:** [Firm] was [lead / sub-consultant]; [partner] delivered [component].
6. **Relevance:** This matters for [current Client] because [specific parallel: user group, regulator, scale, constraint].
7. **Reference:** [Name, role, organisation] has agreed to act as a referee ([contact]).

**Before → after**

- Before: "We have many years of experience delivering successful projects for satisfied clients."
- After: "When outpatient bookings at [client hospital, Mbarara] fell 30% after a rival opened, we weighed price cuts, a radio campaign and WhatsApp booking with SMS reminders. We chose the booking flow because the hospital's problem was access, not price. Within ten weeks bookings were 12% above the earlier baseline and missed appointments fell from 22% to 9%, on the hospital's own records."

## 9. Team

**Job:** show why each person is on this assignment and what they will do. Owned by [`07-team-composition`](../../../pipeline/07-team-composition/SKILL.md).

**Bio formula:** credential or relevant achievement → body of comparable work → recognition or known clients → **what this person will do on this assignment**.

1. [Name], [role], has [n] years in [field] and led [comparable assignment] for [client type]. On this assignment [she / he] will [specific responsibility and deliverable].
2. [Name] will be available for [n] days ([%] of the contract period) and will attend [meetings / field visits].
3. Where the team needs [skill], it is supported by [named adviser], who [relevant record].
4. [Firm] will name a senior sponsor, [name], who will meet [Client]'s [executive] [twice a year / at each phase gate] and resolve escalations within [n] days.
5. Full CVs signed by each expert are at Annex [x].

**Before → after**

- Before: "Sarah is a passionate digital marketing expert with years of experience."
- After: "Sarah Namuli has run paid-search programmes for 14 private hospitals in Uganda and Kenya since 2020. On this assignment she owns the booking-conversion target and the monthly performance review with your marketing manager."

## 10. Work plan and governance

**Job:** make the schedule believable and the working relationship clear. Owned by [`08-work-plan`](../../../pipeline/08-work-plan/SKILL.md).

1. The work runs over [n] weeks in [n] phases; the critical path runs through [dependency].
2. [Firm] and [Client] will meet [weekly / fortnightly] to review [metric]; every meeting has a stated purpose, a named output and a decision to take.
3. Minutes and an action log will reach all participants within two working days.
4. [Client] will provide [data / premises / staff time of n hours per week] by [date]; if this slips, [consequence and recovery].
5. [Firm] will provide [team / tools / licences / training].
6. The schedule includes [n] days of buffer before [milestone], because [risk].

## 11. Deliverables: order and extras

**Job:** match what the client expects first, add extras as options, then tie deliverables to the goal.

**Ordering rule:** list the deliverable the client values most first; add extras under "In addition" and price optional extras separately. If the price is challenged, the client can drop extras without touching the core.

1. [Firm] will deliver to [Client] a [n]-page [report / plan / system], including [component] and [component].
2. Each month [Client] will receive [report] showing [metric], [metric] and [recommended actions].
3. In addition, [Firm] will [extra], so that [benefit].
4. As an optional extra, [Firm] can [add-on], priced separately at [amount] in the financial proposal.
5. These deliverables are the means. The measure of success is [client goal restated].

**Before → after**

- Before: "We will provide a full range of high-quality marketing deliverables."
- After: "Each month [Client hotel, Entebbe] will receive 12 scheduled posts per channel, two short videos, a paid-social report on cost per enquiry and a one-page action note. In addition, we will build a WhatsApp enquiry flow for the wedding team. Together these serve the goal you set: 25 direct wedding enquiries a month by March."

## 12. Cost and value

**Job:** state the price plainly and early, restate what the client gets for it, name what is included beyond that, and close on fit. Owned by [`10-financial-proposal`](../../../pipeline/10-financial-proposal/SKILL.md); respect separate-envelope rules.

**Cost paragraph order:** price → what the client achieves for it → what is included beyond that → fit.

1. The fee for [scope] is [UGX amount], [inclusive / exclusive] of [taxes, to be confirmed through the finance engine], paid in [n] instalments tied to [milestones].
2. [Firm]'s monthly fee for [service] is [amount], invoiced on [date] and payable by [method, including mobile money where agreed].
3. For that fee, [Client] receives [standard scope] and [the specific element the client asked for].
4. [Service] removes about [n] hours of [task] each month and shortens [cycle] from [a] to [b] days ([basis]).
5. Also included: [training / handover / warranty period], so that [benefit] without further agency cost.
6. Each partner's share is [amount]; together the partners reach [shared audience] for less than any could alone.

**Language rules:** use "investment" for strategic work and "fee" for services, and keep the choice consistent; no "only", "just" or "a small investment of"; never pad the sentence before the number.

**Before → after**

- Before: "Our competitive pricing offers excellent value for money. Please find our quote attached."
- After: "The fee is UGX 18,500,000, paid in three milestones. It covers discovery, design, build, content for 14 pages and 60 days of post-launch support. For that fee, [client car dealer, Kira] receives what its current site lacks: enquiries that reach a salesperson within five minutes through WhatsApp routing. Also included is training for two staff to publish stock updates, so the site stays current without further agency cost."

## 13. Pricing options

**Job:** give a real choice without discounting the same scope. Detailed rules: [offer presentation and price order](../../../strategy-positioning/premium-pricing-and-value-defense/references/offer-presentation-and-price-order.md).

1. We offer [two / three] options. They differ in [scope / support level / pace], not in quality controls.
2. **[Essential]** covers [scope] for [amount]. **[Recommended]** adds [x] for [amount]. **[Extended]** adds [y] for [amount].
3. Most clients with [client's situation] choose [option], because [reason] (state only if true and evidenced).
4. A lower fee is possible if we reduce [scope / review cycles / support window]; we would not reduce [control], because [consequence].
5. If you [commit to a 12-month term / provide content by date], then we can [hold current rates / add a quarterly strategy session].

## 14. Guarantees and assurances

**Job:** reduce perceived risk with bounded, lawful promises. Prefer process guarantees to results guarantees.

1. If [milestone] is missed by [date] for reasons within our control, [next month's fee is waived / we extend support at no cost].
2. [Firm] guarantees [service level, e.g. first response within four working hours]; if missed in [n] months of [period], [service credit].
3. If [acceptance test] is not met within [period], [Firm] will [rework at no cost / refund x% of the phase fee].
4. We do not guarantee [ranking / sales / regulatory approval], because [reason]; we do guarantee [the activities and controls that drive it].

Check every guarantee against consumer-protection and contract rules in the target jurisdiction before submission; route pricing and credit mechanics to [`10-financial-proposal`](../../../pipeline/10-financial-proposal/SKILL.md).

## 15. Value beyond the project (closing paragraph)

**Job:** write for the decision-maker above the buyer: the benefit to the whole organisation six months to three years out, why it matters, and what it opens.

1. Beyond [immediate result], [initiative] would give [Client] its first [dataset / capability / asset], which [Client] has never had.
2. That [capability] reduces dependence on [cost / intermediary] and lets [Client] [market directly / plan with evidence].
3. Within [timeframe], it could become the base for [next programme] that no competitor in [region] yet runs.
4. If [pilot] meets [target] by [date], the same approach can extend to [other branches / districts] in [period].

**Before → after**

- Before: "In conclusion, we believe we are the ideal partner and look forward to working with you."
- After: "Beyond filling this season's rooms, a direct-booking engine gives [client resort hotel, Mbale] its own guest database for the first time. That reduces reliance on travel-site commissions and lets the hotel market repeat stays and conferences directly. Within two years it can support a loyalty programme that no competitor in the Elgon region yet runs."

## 16. Closing and next steps

**Job:** ask for one specific action. Never close with a vague invitation. Detailed language: [objection root causes and closing language](../../../strategy-positioning/sales-discovery-and-objection-handling/references/objection-root-cause-and-closing-language.md).

1. There are two sensible ways to start: [option A] or [option B]. Which suits your budget cycle better?
2. Here is how we would start: we sign the engagement letter by [date], hold the inception workshop on [date] and share the first draft by [date].
3. We are booked to walk through this proposal with your team on [day, time]; please bring any questions from finance.
4. Could we agree a decision, either way, by [date]? We will hold the proposed team until then.
5. For a tender: we would welcome the opportunity to clarify any aspect of this proposal at the evaluation committee's convenience.

**Replace these weak closes:** "Let us know your thoughts", "We look forward to hearing from you", "Please do not hesitate to contact us", "What do you think?"

## 17. Words and habits to avoid (proposal-specific)

| Avoid | Use instead |
|---|---|
| "We are pleased to submit…" as an opening | The client's situation or the purpose line |
| "Leading", "world-class", "cutting-edge", "unmatched", "best-in-class" | The evidence: count, named project, result, credential |
| "Tailored", "bespoke", "customised to your unique needs" | The client-specific noun: their members, their sites, their regulator |
| "Robust", "seamless", "holistic", "end-to-end" as filler | What works, under what conditions, and how it is tested |
| "Leverage synergies", "unlock potential", "drive transformation" | The mechanism and the measured change |
| "Extensive experience" | "[n] assignments of this type since [year], listed at Annex [x]" |
| "Competitive pricing" | The price and what it includes |
| "Please find attached" as the whole covering note | One line on what is attached and the decision requested |
| Anonymous testimonials or case studies | Named, permissioned references; if the client cannot be named, say so and describe the client type |
| Unsourced statistics | A sourced figure with date, or no figure |

Run the [anti-ai-slop](../../../meta/anti-ai-slop/SKILL.md) guardrail while drafting and the [ai-slop-audit](../../../meta/ai-slop-audit/SKILL.md) gate after each section.

## 18. Quick rewrite bank

1. "We offer high-quality services at affordable prices." → "Our monthly bookkeeping for Kampala SMEs costs UGX 450,000 and includes EFRIS reconciliation, which many firms charge for separately."
2. "We have many years of experience." → "Since 2019 we have delivered 37 websites for professional-services firms in Uganda and Kenya, 11 of them for law firms."
3. "Our solution is unique." → "Two providers in Uganda offer USSD member statements for SACCOs; we are one of them ([source])."
4. "We will increase your sales." → "The target is 40 additional qualified enquiries a month by Q3, measured through tagged WhatsApp links."
5. "The client needs better marketing." → "In our meeting your sales manager said, 'We don't know which adverts bring customers in.' This proposal answers that first."
6. "We are passionate about customer satisfaction." → "Every complaint is answered within four working hours, and the reply time is reported to you monthly."
7. "This partnership will create synergies." → "Your 60 outlets gain our product line and we gain your delivery routes; neither of us builds what the other already has."
8. "We look forward to a mutually beneficial relationship." → "If the pilot meets the 25-enquiry target by March, the same system can extend to your Gulu and Mbarara branches in the second half of the year."
9. "We can offer a 10% discount." → "If you move to a 24-month term with quarterly volume forecasts, we can reduce the unit rate by 6%, because the forecasts cut our standby costs."
10. "We value our partnership and want to grow together." → "We propose three shared measures for next year, reviewed with your finance director each quarter."

All figures in this bank are illustrative. Replace them with sourced, dated facts from the client file or the Digital Research Engine before use.
