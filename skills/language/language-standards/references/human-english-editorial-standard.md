# Human-English Editorial Standard for Proposals

Parent skill: [Language Standards](../SKILL.md). Related: [proposal phrase bank](../../../writing-content/premium-commercial-writing/references/proposal-phrase-bank.md), [English collocations and lexical precision](english-collocations-and-lexical-precision-2026-09-02.md).

**When to read:** before drafting or reviewing any proposal, bid, EOI, cover letter, executive summary, methodology, case study or evaluator-facing message. `AGENTS.md` makes this standard mandatory for evaluator-facing text. It governs reader, purpose, genre, grammar, collocation, rhythm and proof.

This is the engine's own editorial standard (adopted 2026-08-27, moved to this location 2026-09-23). It was informed by general grammar, vocabulary and exam-writing practice texts used only as concept inputs; no framework in it is attributed to a single book.

## Proposal standard

Write for the evaluator's actual decision. Human proposal writing is tailored, evidence-bearing and candid about delivery conditions. It should sound like a capable professional who has understood the assignment, not like a machine filling headings.

## Five passes

1. **Frame:** name the evaluator, procurement setting, purpose, decision and mandatory constraints.
2. **Journey:** build the evaluator journey: need; understanding; method; deliverables; team; schedule; risk; proof; value; next step.
3. **Evidence:** map every material claim to proposer evidence, ToR language or verified external evidence. Label assumptions and gaps.
4. **Revise:** direct sentences, exact verbs, varied rhythm, parallel lists, correct grammar, and clear links between activities and outputs.
5. **Proof:** check compliance, names, dates, prices, cross-references, forms, headings and envelope separation; read aloud; run the proposal-specific gates.

## Craft rules

- British English and East African professional register are the default unless the buyer specifies otherwise.
- State the evaluator-relevant point early. Replace prestige adjectives with scope, proof, method, measure, owner or boundary.
- Use rare vocabulary only when sense, collocation and register are exact.
- Check agreement, articles, tense and aspect, pronouns, relative clauses, prepositions, parallelism, modifiers, punctuation, spelling and sentence boundaries.
- Give each paragraph a point, support and implication. Do not enforce a fixed sentence count, but lead scored sections with a power paragraph (see the [power-paragraph method](../../../writing-content/premium-commercial-writing/references/proposal-power-paragraph-method.md)).
- For major promises, privately test **claim → evidence → warrant → countercase → implication**.
- Use transitions to show logic, not to decorate every paragraph. Vary rhythm because the thought varies.
- Classification and glossary discipline: define a term once, use it the same way throughout, and keep controlled vocabularies (phase names, deliverable names, role titles) identical across sections.
- Confusable-word check: affect/effect, principal/principle, complement/compliment, ensure/insure, practice/practise, licence/license, dependant/dependent, stationary/stationery.

## Anti-slop questions

- Could another consultant submit this paragraph unchanged? Add the ToR's actual language, the client's context, the delivery mechanism, named proof or a real constraint, or cut it.
- Does the method explain who does what, when, with what evidence?
- Is there fake warmth, inflated capability, a stock conclusion or an invented credential? Remove it.
- Never add mistakes to imitate a human writer.

## Evidence record

Record buyer and purpose, ToR and profile, claim-proof map, terms checked, revision and proof status, unresolved gaps, reviewer and date. Editorial polish never substitutes for compliance or evidence.
