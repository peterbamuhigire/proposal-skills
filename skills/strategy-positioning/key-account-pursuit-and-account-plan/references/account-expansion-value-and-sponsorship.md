# Account Expansion, Value Co-Creation and Sponsorship

Parent skill: [Key-Account Pursuit and Account Plan](../SKILL.md).

**When to read:** at workflow steps 6 and 7, to plan growth inside the account, design value the client can measure, and govern the account with a sponsor and a team.

Sources (methods only, paraphrased): Account Cube, external service values and onboarding from Hunter, V. L. with Tietyen, D. (1997) *Business to Business Marketing: Creating a Community of Customers*, NTC Business Books; value-based selling, co-creation, value assessment, sponsor roles and team design as presented in Marcos, J. et al. (c. 2025) *The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page; underselling review from Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press.

## 1. Account Cube: planning growth inside an account

The Account Cube (attributed above) orders expansion from the closest opportunity to the furthest. Use it as a planning grid, not a sales script:

| Direction | Question to answer | Typical entry route |
|---|---|---|
| Deeper with the same buyer | Which other service lines solve a problem this buyer already owns? | Quarterly review with the current contact |
| Wider across the same buying group | Who else has the same responsibility and the same problem? | Introduction from the satisfied contact |
| Across functions at the same site | Which other function has a related problem we can evidence? | Sponsor or executive introduction |
| Across sites, subsidiaries or countries | Where else in the group does the same problem exist? | Group-level sponsor and a documented result |

Rules for the grid: work outward only after results are visible at the current level; record every cell as bought, open or blocked (with the reason); attach a named business problem to every proposed step, because a wish that solves no problem is not a need; and ask for introductions explicitly: "Who else in [department or site] deals with [problem] and might benefit from what we did for your team?"

Illustration (fictional): a regional fintech buys community management only. The next steps might be paid acquisition for the same team, then the second product team, then employer-brand content for HR, then the subsidiary in a neighbouring country, each introduced after a quarterly review against the client's own measures.

**Share-of-wallet (underselling) review, quarterly:** estimate the client's total spend in our category, compute our share, list unmet needs, prepare a fuller recommendation with options, and ask for it directly. Proposals should prescribe the full solution the client needs, with options, not the smallest safe scope.

## 2. Why clients buy from us (external service values)

A few attributes usually explain most buying behaviour in a segment. Find them before writing win themes:

1. **Ask new clients** soon after the first purchase why they chose us over the alternatives.
2. **Interview lost or departed clients.** Classify why they left: price, service, product, organisational change, the client's market, or a substitute. Do not spend to save the unsalvageable types.
3. **Feature-set comparison:** list every service, operational and commercial attribute we offer (reporting frequency, response time, languages, payment terms including mobile money); score competitors on the same list; strike out table stakes; mark where we are uniquely better and where rivals are better; ask five to ten clients to rank the shortlist **and** rate how much each item affects their buying decision. An attribute ranked high but with low buying importance is not a win theme.

Keep only attributes backed by a real, repeatable capability.

## 3. Value-based selling and co-creation

**Product pitch versus value pitch**

- Product pitch: "Our platform processes 10,000 records a second and integrates with most ERPs."
- Value pitch: "From your own transaction data, we estimate [solution] could cut stock-outs by [x]% and release about UGX [amount] of working capital a year. We will agree the baseline with your finance team in the first 30 days and review it with you each quarter."

**Three value-based selling tasks:** understand the client's business model and value drivers; build a value proposition tailored to them; quantify and communicate value in their metrics with a baseline and a measurement method.

**Readiness for co-creation:** mutual trust, shared goals, committed resources on both sides, and willingness to adapt. In genuine co-creation the client contributes comparable effort.

**Co-creation sequence (usable as methodology phases):** co-diagnosis (frame the problem together) → co-ideation → co-design → co-testing → co-evaluation.

**Four levers for sustained co-creation:** frequent open interaction; pooled resources and knowledge; joint investigation of problems; joint innovation, such as pricing aligned to the client's seasonal cash flow.

**Value-assessment cycle (four stages):**

1. Identify value potential: explicit needs, processes, financial implications.
2. Baseline: current performance, agreed outcomes, trial runs.
3. Evaluate performance: measured impact on the client's results.
4. Realise and document long-term value: verified, attributed and recorded (this record becomes the next case study).

**Value types to evidence:** top-line (sales, coverage), bottom-line (cost, waste, labour), reputation and continuity (incidents, rejects), strategic (new market, diversification).

**Quarterly value-tracking agenda:** baseline recap → measures that moved → value realised and attributed → issues and recovery actions → new opportunities → joint priorities next quarter → decisions needed.

## 4. Onboarding a new key client

The first call after award is the most valuable call. Within five working days:

1. Thank the client and check that nothing has gone wrong.
2. Confirm priorities, reporting preferences and who else should receive updates.
3. Grade the client and assign the contact plan.
4. Hand over to the people who will run the account, without a seam.
5. Note the likely second need and the first Account Cube step.

## 5. Executive sponsor

**Roles a sponsor can play:** relationship builder (senior-to-senior social contact), strategic visionary, resource allocator, organisational aligner, knowledge facilitator and coach, cultural champion, performance monitor, crisis manager, decision-maker. The last two carry risks: short-termism and bottlenecks that disempower the account lead.

**Operating rules**

- Senior involvement works best when it is social and strategic; tactical interventions can signal lack of confidence in the account lead.
- Agree the commitment at kick-off: indicative hours a year, a monthly check-in with the account lead, and the moments the sponsor attends (annual review, launches, crises).
- The account lead has a say in who is chosen and stays visibly in charge.
- Plan sponsor succession.
- In relationship-centred, hierarchy-conscious markets (common in East African family groups and parastatals), plan owner-to-owner or principal-to-principal contact around calendar moments (year-end gatherings, AGMs) while keeping the account lead in charge; validate this with local experience.

**Sponsor request script:** "I would like you to sponsor [account]. It is worth [amount] a year with [growth potential]. I am asking for about [n] hours a year: two senior conversations with their MD, a short monthly check-in with me, and your backing when we need [resource]."

**Sponsor briefing pack (per meeting):** context; objective for the meeting; attendees with GRASP notes; talking points; topics to avoid (no tactical pricing); desired next step.

**Escalation note (always bring options):** issue → impact on the account → options with pros and cons → recommendation → decision needed by [date].

**Account report to leadership (monthly or quarterly):** measures, wins, opportunities, challenges, decisions required, resources needed.

## 6. The account team

- Size the core team at about five to nine people across commercial, delivery, finance and digital skills.
- Assign a RASIC per phase (responsible, accountable, supporting, informing, contributing): opportunity identification, qualification, solution design, proposal, negotiation, delivery, review.
- Check the team's working-style mix (coordinators, specialists, completers, idea generators) so that planning, delivery and challenge are all covered.
- Quarterly team health pulse: Do we admit mistakes? Do we debate strategy? Are we committed to the plan? Do we hold each other to account? Do we put the client's results first?
- Reward shared account outcomes as well as individual targets, so the team does not optimise functions against the client.
