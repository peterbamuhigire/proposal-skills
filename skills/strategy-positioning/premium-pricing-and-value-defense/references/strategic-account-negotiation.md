# Strategic-Account Negotiation

## 1. Premises

- The future relationship matters as much as the current deal; there is mutual dependence.
- A negotiation is the exchange of conditional concessions to reach an agreement that serves each party's interests, weighs cost against value, and keeps the relationship working.
- In long relationships the real negotiation often continues after signature.

## 2. Negotiation procedure for a strategic client

Work through the procedure in this order. It is this engine's own sequence; the practices behind it are attributed above.

**Step A. Build the negotiation file (at least two working days before the meeting)**

- Write down what the client needs from this deal and what it cannot concede, then do the same for us.
- Record our walk-away position and our best alternative if there is no agreement.
- Set every variable as a range (target, acceptable, walk-away), never a single figure.
- List where our priorities differ from the client's; different priorities are where value can be traded (for example, faster implementation may be worth more to the client than a discount).
- Fill in the concession worksheet (section 4) and pre-write the conditional wording.
- Cast the team: who leads, who speaks on technical points, who values concessions, who watches the room, who takes notes. Agree signals for a break.

**Step B. Open on common ground**

- Start with the interests both sides share and settle easy points first to build momentum.
- Win agreement on the solution and its value before any discussion of price.
- When you must disagree, give the reason first and ask a question rather than rejecting outright; do not announce "I disagree".

**Step C. Answer price pressure with structure, not discounts**

- Move the discussion to total cost of ownership and to the value delivered to the client's own customers.
- Look for cost that can be removed from the whole system, including the client's suppliers, logistics or operations teams, rather than cutting our unit price.
- Offer restructured packages of equal value to us (section 3) instead of a lower price for the same scope.
- Where value is uncertain, propose a contingent term: a stage gate, a performance-linked element or a success fee tied to measured outcomes. Route outcome-pricing mechanics to the outcome-pricing skills where relevant.
- Where risk genuinely sits on both sides, allocate it explicitly in the terms (measures, exposure limits, dispute route) and keep any profit-share formula transparent.

**Step D. Trade, never give**

- Every concession is labelled as a concession and made conditional: "If you…, then we…".
- Offer small items before large ones, and make each later concession smaller than the one before.
- Give what is cheap for us and valuable to the client; ask for what is cheap for the client and valuable to us.
- Do not over-explain a concession.

**Step E. After signature**

- Treat the signed agreement as the fallback, then offer a short joint review to look for improvements that must benefit both sides (timing, payment, extra services, introductions).
- Keep reinforcing value beyond price: ease of working together, peace of mind, the client's own ambitions.
- In long relationships, expect the negotiation to continue through delivery; keep a record of every agreed change.

## 3. Multiple equivalent simultaneous offers

Present two or three packages together that are **of equal value to us** but differ in price, scope, support and risk-sharing. The client's preference reveals its priorities and breaks deadlock.

| Package | Shape |
|---|---|
| A | Higher fee, full support, fixed scope, fastest timeline |
| B | Mid fee, standard support, additional capacity or licences |
| C | Lower base fee, usage- or success-linked element, lighter support |

Script: "We have prepared three options of equal value to us. They differ in how much support and risk-sharing you want. Which comes closest to how you would like to work?"

Record which option the client leans to and why.

## 5. Language bank

- Inclusive opening: "It is encouraging that we both want [shared interest]. Could we start there before we come to [difficult issue]?"
- Conditional concession: "If you can commit to [12-month term / quarterly forecasts / reference rights], then we can [hold current rates / add a quarterly strategy session]."
- Disagreeing without labelling it: "Here is what our delivery data shows: [reason 1], [reason 2]. Given that, how would a [x]-week timeline protect your launch date?"
- Declining a same-scope discount: "We can reach a lower fee by changing [scope / support window / review cycles]. We would not recommend removing [control], because [consequence]."
- Rewrite of a weak concession: instead of "We can offer a 10% discount", say "If you move to a 24-month term with quarterly volume forecasts, we can reduce the unit rate by 6%, because the forecasts cut our standby costs."
- Post-settlement: "Our agreement stands whatever happens in the next half hour. Could we use that time to look for anything that would make it better for both of us, such as timing, payment, extra services or introductions?"
- Silence: after stating a proposal, stop and let the other side respond.

## 6. Behaviours that work

- Ask questions to gather information, control pace and test assertions.
- Acknowledge emotions without conceding substance.
- Insist on objective criteria (market data, benchmarks, the client's own figures).
- Treat people the way they want to be treated; adjust to high-context, hierarchy-conscious settings with more relationship time and indirect framing.
- Know your own anger and fear triggers; take breaks; rehearse the difficult moments.

## 7. Ethics filter (excluded tactics)

- Do not exaggerate problems or complexity to raise perceived value ("issue amplification").
- Do not slip extra services or terms into an agreement without clearly stating them.
- Do not invent deadlines, competing offers or scarcity.
- Do not use contract enforcement as a first move; reserve it for serious breaches, after internal alignment, documented and with a recovery plan.

## 8. Renewal pattern for retainers

1. Open with results against the agreed measures and the next-year plan (value first).
2. Present equivalent-value packages.
3. Trade concessions conditionally.
4. Close with the specific next step and document everything.
5. Hold a short post-settlement conversation, for example exchanging case-study rights for a quarterly workshop.
