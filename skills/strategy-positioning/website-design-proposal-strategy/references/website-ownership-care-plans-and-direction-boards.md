# Website Ownership, Care Plans and Direction Boards

Parent skill: [Website Design Proposal Strategy](../SKILL.md). Companion gate: [website design proposal gate](website-design-proposal-gate.md).

**When to read:** when a website proposal needs ownership commitments, a purpose statement, a care or maintenance plan, a creative-direction deliverable, owner-education answers, or honest search expectations. Delivery doctrine stays with `website-skills`; visual doctrine with `design-system-skills` (resolve both through the global routing table). This reference sets what the proposal promises and prices.

Sources (methods only, paraphrased): purpose split, asset ownership, owner questions, care-plan housekeeping and SEO expectation-setting from Plumley, G. (2011) *Website Design and Development: 100 Questions to Ask Before Building a Website*, Wiley; direction-board protocol and style cost tiers from McNeil, P. (2010, 2013) *The Web Designer's Idea Book*, Volumes 2 and 3, HOW Books; build-inside-retainer pattern from Nelson, J. (2019) *The Seven Figure Agency Roadmap*. All prices, screen sizes and platform facts from those books are obsolete and excluded.

## 1. Purpose split (first page of the proposal's understanding section)

Ask "Why does [Client] want this website?" and record two lists:

| Outcome the site must achieve | Tasks the site must perform | Page or feature | Measure |
|---|---|---|---|
| More WhatsApp enquiries from Kampala corporates | Click-to-WhatsApp, service pages, proof | Service pages, contact bar | Tagged enquiries per month |
| Credibility for tenders and NGO partners | Profile, registrations, past projects, downloadable capability statement | About, projects, media kit | Downloads; shortlist feedback |
| Diaspora bookings | UGX and USD pricing, trust signals, time-zone-aware contact | Booking flow | Bookings from abroad |

Every proposed feature must trace to an outcome. A one-page presence is a legitimate, deliberate starting point when chosen on purpose. This table becomes the acceptance backbone for launch.

## 2. Ownership commitments ("you own everything")

Put these commitments in the methodology and the acceptance criteria:

1. The client is the **registrant** of every domain, using the client's name and a durable email address that does not depend on the domain itself. The agency may be listed as technical contact only.
2. The client owns the content, design files, code written for the client, analytics properties, business profiles, social and advertising accounts, and the WhatsApp Business number, subject to third-party licence terms, which are listed.
3. Credentials for domain, hosting, CMS, email and analytics are separate; contractors get their own accounts, removed at handover; all shared passwords are rotated at handover.
4. An **asset ownership and access register** is delivered at kick-off and handover: asset, provider, owner of record, technical contact, login owner, two-factor method, renewal date, auto-renew, cost, who pays, recovery email, last verified.
5. For hosted or turnkey platforms, the proposal states exactly what the client can take away if it leaves (usually content and data, not the platform's design or code).
6. Clause wording: "You will be the registered owner of [domain] and every account we create for you. We are listed as technical contact only, so we can help without holding the keys. If you move to another provider, here is what goes with you: [list]."

**Red flag to address in writing:** a current domain registered to a former designer, staff member or volunteer. Scope a recovery step (lookup, contact, registrar process with proof of engagement) and check the ccTLD registry's current rules (.ug, .co.ke) before promising a timeline.

## 3. Design-route options (honest total-cost comparison)

| Route | Strength | Hidden cost or risk |
|---|---|---|
| Custom design and build | Fit to purpose and brand; advice on alternatives | Higher fee; depends on implementation skill |
| Template plus professional customisation | Lower cost; proven structure | Layout compromises; code quality to check |
| Hosted turnkey platform | Fast; low starting cost | Limited flexibility; design and code stay with the platform |
| Client does it alone | No labour fee | Time cost, learning curve, quality risk |

Offer a "template plus customisation" tier for smaller budgets rather than refusing the client.

## 4. Paid creative-direction boards

For premium work, sell creative direction as a paid discovery deliverable:

- **Standard:** one direction. **Premium discovery tier:** three directions (fit, stretch, bold).
- Each board contains: a one-sentence style thesis ("For [audience] doing [job], this route signals [belief] because [reason]"); type character and chosen typeface (per design-system anti-slop rules); palette logic with an accessibility check; imagery brief (locally shot where possible); one hero mock using the client's real headline and image; one component sample; a mobile view on a mid-range Android phone; build and maintenance cost tiers; risks.
- **Acceptance:** the client selects one route against recorded criteria; the decision and rejected routes are logged.
- **Style cost tiers:** tag each proposed style or effect with a build tier and a maintenance tier, and answer "Who can change this without a designer?" This is also a scope-creep control.

Visual execution routes to `design-system-skills`.

## 5. Care plans (what happens after launch)

Price post-launch care as a named programme, never as an implied free service. A care plan specifies:

| Area | Commitment to specify |
|---|---|
| Backups | What is backed up (files, database, form data, mailing list), frequency, off-server storage plus a second location, date-named, regular restore test |
| Monthly functional check | Every page, links, forms (including error handling and the recipient mailbox), embedded media, payment and booking flows |
| Updates and security | CMS, plugin and dependency updates; removal of unused software; security scan; admin-access hygiene |
| Content updates | Three streams: new items (news, blog), new pages, revisions to existing pages; hours included per month |
| Contact-information audit | Registrar, hosting and CMS contact emails; every page and form holding contact details, including WhatsApp numbers that change with staff turnover |
| Monitoring and reporting | Uptime monitoring; monthly report with measures tied to the purpose statement |
| Twice-yearly review | Revisit purpose, content and priorities with the owner |

Offer tiers (for example essential, growth, managed) that differ in hours and response times, not in backups or security. For clients who decline a care plan, provide a short **self-care handbook** and the asset register, which protects both the client and the agency's reputation.

Pair the build with a fast-signal growth component where marketing is in scope (see [offer presentation and price order](../../premium-pricing-and-value-defense/references/offer-presentation-and-price-order.md)).

## 6. Owner questions answered before they are asked

Pre-answer these in the proposal or an annex:

- Who will own the domain and accounts? (section 2)
- Can we leave with our content and files? What goes with us?
- Who backs up the site, how often, and have restores been tested?
- Who can edit content without a developer, and how are they trained?
- What happens when a page moves? (permanent redirects and a helpful not-found page)
- What will we need to provide, and by when?
- What is excluded from the fee (hosting, licences, photography, copywriting, translation)?

## 7. Honest search and AI-search expectations

- Never promise rankings; treat any "guaranteed page one" offer as a red flag and say why.
- Search effects on a new site take time; commit to activities and measures, not positions.
- Per Google (checked 2026-09, claim CW-07), there is no special markup, llms.txt or "AI optimisation" requirement for AI Overviews or AI Mode; people-first content and standard technical SEO apply.
- Do not sell FAQ or HowTo structured data as a rich-result tactic: FAQ rich results stopped showing on 7 May 2026 and HowTo results are no longer shown (claim CW-09). Check Google's current search gallery before promising any rich result.
- Mass-produced low-value pages risk scaled-content-abuse action (claim CW-08); one page per service per city only where the client genuinely serves that place and can prove it.
- Performance and accessibility targets: cite Core Web Vitals thresholds (claim CW-01) and WCAG 2.2 Level AA (claim CW-05) with their check dates; do not claim WCAG 3 conformance.

## 8. Standard page inventory for scoping

Home (two primary actions, a featured slot the owner can change, no splash page), service pages (one per service), overview page, about (story, not sales pitch), team write-ups, contact (all routes, map, hours, directions by landmark, premises photo from the road), FAQ (genuinely frequent questions), media kit (logos, bios, images, profile PDF), testimonials and case studies (named and permissioned), privacy notice and terms (referencing the applicable data-protection law; legal review), custom not-found page, site map where the site exceeds about ten pages. Content drafting is scoped and priced explicitly.
