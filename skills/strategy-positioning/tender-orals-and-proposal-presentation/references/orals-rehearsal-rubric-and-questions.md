# Orals Rehearsal, Rubric and Questions

Parent skill: [Tender Orals and Proposal Presentation](../SKILL.md).

**When to read:** at workflow steps 5, 7, 9 and 10, to build the hard-questions pack, score rehearsals, handle questions on the day and debrief.

Sources (methods only, paraphrased): the evaluation rubric and after-action review from Kupsh, J. and Graves, P. R. (1993) *How to Create High-Impact Business Presentations*, NTC Business Books; hostile-question handling and steel-manning from Lin, L. C. (2013) *Decode and Conquer*, Impact Interview; tough-question rehearsal from Maltz, M. et al. (1998) *Zero-Resistance Selling*, Prentice Hall Press. The rubric weights below are reweighted for tender orals by this engine.

## 1. Rehearsal protocol

1. First full run-through at least five working days before the event; second at least two days before.
2. Two colleagues who did not write the proposal score each run with the rubric (section 2), independently.
3. Time every section and the hand-offs between presenters.
4. Run a hostile panel round: reviewers ask the hardest questions from the pack in their toughest form.
5. Record fixes, re-time, and re-run the weakest section.
6. The day before: preview the deck order, refresh facts and figures, rest.

## 2. Orals rubric (50 points)

| Area | Points | What earns the points |
|---|---:|---|
| Responsiveness to criteria | 10 | Each scoring criterion is addressed where the panel expects it; the client's problem is stated in its terms; nothing contradicts the written proposal |
| Content and proof | 10 | Technically correct; right level of detail; claims supported by named evidence; numbers sourced |
| Organisation | 10 | Purpose stated early; structure signposted; logical order; smooth hand-offs; conclusion tied to the opening and ending with a clear request |
| Visuals and materials | 10 | Slides support rather than repeat; charts honest and legible; handout useful; equipment handled without distraction |
| Delivery and Q&A | 10 | Calm command; eye contact; natural pace and voice; questions repeated, answered briefly and checked; gaps admitted with follow-up |

Bands: 45–50 ready; 38–44 fix named issues; below 38 rework and re-rehearse. Record reviewer names, date and fixes.

## 3. Hard-questions pack

For each question: the question in its hardest form, the short answer (two or three sentences), the evidence behind it, the owner, and what not to say.

**Question families to cover**

- Price: "Why are you more expensive than [competitor type]?" "What would you drop for 15% less?"
- Team: "Is [key expert] really available for the whole period?" "Who replaces them if they leave?"
- Method: "Why this approach and not [alternative]?" "What happens if the data is not available in month one?"
- Risk: "What is the biggest risk to this assignment, and what have you done about it?"
- Experience: "Which of your cited projects is most like ours, and what went wrong on it?"
- Local reality: "How will you work in districts with poor connectivity?" "How do you handle approvals that take longer than planned?"
- Commercial terms: payment schedule, retention, liability, exit.
- The steel-man question: "What is the strongest argument against choosing you?" Prepare an honest answer and why the panel should still choose you.

## 4. Handling questions on the day

1. **Repeat or paraphrase the question** so the whole panel hears it: "The question is whether [x]."
2. **Answer in one or two sentences,** then add evidence only if needed.
3. **Check:** "Does that answer your question?"
4. **Assign the right presenter** without passing the question around the table.
5. **Admit gaps:** "I do not have that figure with me. We will confirm it in writing by [day], if the procedure allows."
6. **Hostile or flawed questions:** separate the question from the emotion; if the premise is flawed, say so courteously, offer the better framing, and still answer the original question.
7. **Read the room:** many questions can mean interest or a gap in coverage; no questions can mean full coverage or disengagement. Adjust.
8. **Never add new commitments** on price, staff or scope in the room; offer to clarify in writing through the procurement channel.

## 5. Opening and closing patterns

- Opening: "[Chairperson / protocol] observed. Thank you for the opportunity. [Client] needs [outcome] by [date], and the hard part is [overriding question]. In the next [n] minutes we will show how we would solve it, who will do it, and how you will know it is working."
- Signposting: "We will cover three things: the approach, the team and how we control risk."
- Close: "Beyond [immediate result], this gives [Client] [future capability]. We would welcome the opportunity to start on [date] and have the team ready from [date]."
- For private pitches, end with the choice or organised close in the [closing language reference](../../sales-discovery-and-objection-handling/references/objection-root-cause-and-closing-language.md).

## 6. Same-day after-action review

- Three things that worked.
- Three things to improve.
- Questions asked, answers given, and those not answered well.
- Follow-ups owed, with owners and dates.
- Handout uptake and any materials left behind.
- Rubric score summary and changes for the next orals.

Record the review in the proposal file and feed lasting lessons into the Kaizen cycle (`meta/kaizen-improvement-system`).
