# Marketing and Digital-Marketing Service Proposals

Parent skill: [Premium Client Proposal Strategy](../SKILL.md). Related: [offer presentation and price order](../../premium-pricing-and-value-defense/references/offer-presentation-and-price-order.md), [website design proposal strategy](../../website-design-proposal-strategy/SKILL.md).

**When to read:** when the proposal sells marketing strategy, a marketing plan, digital marketing, social media management, paid advertising, content, influencer programmes, or a combined website-and-marketing retainer. This reference governs the **proposal** (win logic, offer, scope, commercial terms, handoffs). The marketing substance itself belongs to other engines.

## 1. Cross-engine handoffs (who owns what)

| Need inside the proposal | Owner engine | What proposal-skills hands over | What comes back into the proposal |
|---|---|---|---|
| A marketing plan as a deliverable, or the marketing section of a business plan | `business-plan-skills` (marketing-plan orchestrator) | Client brief, objectives, budget ceiling, markets, evidence gaps | Plan structure, SMART location-specific objectives, budget logic to describe in the methodology |
| Digital marketing, social media, paid advertising, content calendars, influencer and community work | `social-media-skills` (being repositioned as the digital marketing and advertising engine) | Approved scope, audience, channels named by the client, measurement expectations, commercial boundaries | Channel strategy, content and campaign method, platform playbooks, reporting design |
| Website build, SEO, conversion, care plans | `website-skills` | Approved scope, purpose split, ownership commitments, acceptance criteria | Delivery method, quality gates, launch checklist |
| Visual layout of the proposal document or direction boards | `design-system-skills` | Content and structure | Typography, layout, colour decisions |
| Current platform facts (ad formats, objectives, policies) | `digital-research-engine` | Claims to verify | Dated, sourced claims |
| Tax on ad spend, VAT on foreign platforms, pricing arithmetic | `chwezi-accounting-doctrine` | Cost lines and jurisdiction | Confirmed treatment |

Resolve engine locations through the global routing table. The proposal must not restate another engine's doctrine; it describes the approach at proposal depth and cites the deliverable.

## 2. Win logic for marketing proposals

1. **Start from the business outcome,** not the channel list: enquiries, bookings, admissions, deposits, footfall, repeat purchase, with a baseline.
2. **Customer understanding before creative.** Separate who the customer is from what they want; commit to finding motivations, purchase triggers and objections.
3. **One message, few channels.** Concentrate budget where the audience already looks (WhatsApp groups, radio, church and association networks, search, specific platforms) rather than spreading thinly.
4. **Low-cost, high-trust tactics first** (referral incentives, partner displays, community events, opted-in WhatsApp broadcasts), with paid spend scaled only after conversion evidence.
5. **Measurement honesty.** Never promise reach, engagement, conversions or rankings without data. Distinguish documented workflow from observed result; state what will be measured, how and from when.

## 3. Engagement architecture (sellable sequence)

| Stage | Commercial form | Output | Gate to next stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer insight report | Fixed fee | Segments, motivations, triggers, objections, media map, sample and method notes | Client accepts findings |
| Strategic marketing plan | Fixed fee | Objectives, positioning, message, channel and budget plan, measures | Plan approved; funnel fixes agreed |
| Test sprint | Fixed fee plus test media budget | Tested messages and offers, cost per qualified lead by channel | Results meet agreed thresholds |
| Launch and optimise retainer | Monthly, with notice period | Campaigns, content, reporting, monthly review | Quarterly value review |

This sequence stops the agency being hired to "just run ads" on a website or sales process that cannot convert. Where the client wants only one stage, state the risks of skipping the others.

## 4. Retainer design rules

- Two or three defined programmes per service line; no à-la-carte builds.
- Pair a fast-signal component (paid campaign, enquiry flow) with slow-compounding work (content, search, community) so evidence arrives before doubts about return set in, typically around the third or fourth month.
- Anchor each quarter to one agreed primary measure with a few guard-rail measures, rather than a long report.
- State the client's obligations (approvals within [n] days, content supply, access to analytics and sales data) and what happens if they slip.
- Monthly review agenda: work done → results against measures → next 30/60/90 days → seasonal plans → client actions → next review date.
- Onboarding: set-up sheet (access, assets, contacts, approver), launch call with the writer present, and a written 90-day plan.

## 5. Creative-approach section (when creative work is proposed)

- State the criteria the creative will be judged against before showing ideas.
- Offer at least three genuinely different ideas or routes, each tied to the criteria.
- Name who decides (decision-maker clause) and how feedback will be given.
- Be honest about budget: explain what the production budget buys and itemise assets.
- Explain line items that look expensive with the reason and the execution realism behind them.

## 6. Influencer and creator programmes (commercial terms)

Before any contract is drafted, complete a term sheet covering: deliverables and formats, posting window, archive or removal terms, exclusivity scope and duration, usage rights and tiers, payment terms, travel, disclosure (use clear "ad" labelling as best practice; no Uganda- or Kenya-specific influencer-disclosure law was found as at 2026-09-23, claim AD-10, and FTC and UK rules apply to those audiences, claim AD-09), minors' consent, data protection, conduct clause, and dispute venue. Legal drafting goes to a qualified lawyer.

## 7. Compliance points that affect scope and price

- Direct marketing and prospecting lists: Uganda requires consent to collect personal data and honours written objections to direct marketing within 14 days (claim PL-01); Kenya requires consent and a free, simple opt-out and treats objections as absolute (claim PL-02). Never promise purchased or scraped lists.
- Special ad categories (housing, employment, financial products, social issues and politics) restrict targeting in some markets; Meta no longer accepts political or social-issue ads in the EU since October 2025 (claim AD-03). Verify before promising targeting.
- EU-, UK- or Swiss-facing campaigns need Consent Mode v2 through a consent platform (claim CW-10).
- Platform objectives, formats and specifications change; cite only dated, registered facts (claims AD-01 to AD-08) or phrase them as checks.

## 8. Decision memo for recommendations inside retainers

When a client must choose (budget change, competitive response, channel shift), use a one-page memo: purpose → three to five key facts → about three markedly different options including "do nothing" → recommendation with reasons → action plan (money, people, dates) → optional contingency. Decide at reasonable confidence; do not recommend "more research" to avoid a decision.

## 9. Phrase patterns

- "For the first six months, [Firm] will focus on the [n] [households / businesses] in [areas] that already [behaviour], using three low-cost tactics: [tactic 1], [tactic 2], [tactic 3]. The target is [n] [repeat customers / enquiries] by [date], at an acquisition cost below [amount]."
- "We will not promise reach or engagement figures before the test sprint. We will promise the measures, the method and a monthly review, and we will report what we observe."
- "Paid spend will increase only after [tactic] shows a cost per qualified enquiry below [amount] for [n] consecutive weeks."
