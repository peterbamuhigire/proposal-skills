# Case-Study and Past-Performance Write-Up Structure

Parent skill: [05 Relevant Experience](../SKILL.md). Related: [proposal phrase bank](../../../writing-content/premium-commercial-writing/references/proposal-phrase-bank.md) section 8.

**When to read:** when writing project cards, capability statements, past-performance narratives, case studies, award entries or proof blocks inside other sections; and when setting up a new engagement so that it will produce a usable case study later.

Sources (methods only, paraphrased): the stakes → options → actions → impact story structure and the credibility tests from Lin, L. C. (2013) *Decode and Conquer*, 2nd edn, Impact Interview (the trademarked method name is not used); pre-deployment baselines from Croll, A. and Yoskovitz, B. (2013) *Lean Analytics*, O'Reilly Media; testimonial and case-study permission practice from Plumley, G. (2011) *Website Design and Development*, Wiley; value-realisation records from Marcos, J. et al. (c. 2025) *The High-Performing Key Account Manager*, Kogan Page.

## 1. Four-part story

| Part | Write | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Stakes | The client, its situation and why it mattered: the baseline, the risk, the deadline | Would the reader understand what was lost if nothing changed? |
| Options | The two or three responses considered and why they were weighed | Does it show judgement, not just activity? |
| Actions | What our team did, first-hand and specifically: who, what, the resistance met | Could a reader tell our role from the client's and partners'? |
| Impact | The result against the baseline, with time frame and evidence source; if no number exists, a named client statement | Is the result verified, dated and attributed? |

Close with **relevance**: one sentence on why this example matters for the current client (same user group, regulator, scale, constraint or service model).

## 2. Credibility tests before a case is used

- **Owner or participant?** State exactly whether the firm led, co-delivered or sub-consulted, and which partner delivered which part.
- **Good or great?** Ask what would have happened without us; present the counterfactual honestly.
- **Baseline present?** A result without a starting point is weak proof.
- **Permission?** Named clients and quoted people have agreed in writing. If the client cannot be named, describe the client type and consider whether the anonymous case still persuades; anonymity raises doubt.
- **Current?** Evidence older than twelve months is re-checked; date every proof item.
- **Comparable?** Omit projects that are not genuinely comparable; they weaken the section.

## 3. Project card (tender format)

```
CLIENT:      [Organisation], [Country]            (or client type, if confidential)
ASSIGNMENT:  [Title]
PERIOD:      [Month/Year – Month/Year]
OUR ROLE:    [Lead / Co-lead / Sub-consultant]; partners: [names and parts]
STAKES:      [Situation and baseline in one or two sentences]
APPROACH:    [Options weighed and the choice made, one sentence]
WHAT WE DID: [Two or three specific actions]
RESULTS:     • [Metric: baseline → result, time frame, source]
             • [Second result]
RELEVANCE:   [Why this matters for the current assignment]
REFERENCE:   [Name, role, contact] (permission recorded [date])
```

## 4. Building case studies into every engagement

1. **Capture baselines before implementation.** Record three to five operational measures from the client's own records (for example orders per day, response time, cost per lead, days from referral to first visit) with date and source.
2. **Agree the measurement method** with the client's finance or operations lead.
3. **Report deltas** at agreed intervals (for example after 90 days) against the client's own numbers, not industry averages.
4. **Record realised value** in the quarterly review, attributed and signed off by the client.
5. **Ask for permission and a quote at the moment of success.** Offer to draft the quote from what the client has said, for approval or editing.
6. **Store the case** with its evidence, permission record and review date in the proof library.

## 5. Formats beyond the tender

- **Capability statement paragraph:** stakes → what we did → result → what it made possible next.
- **Client endorsement:** in the client's voice, calm rather than superlative, with permission and review rights.
- **Orals:** tell one case story in under a minute, with the result against the baseline; see [tender orals](../../../strategy-positioning/tender-orals-and-proposal-presentation/SKILL.md).

## 6. Rewrite example (illustrative)

- Before: "We successfully delivered a booking system for a leading hospital, improving patient experience."
- After: "When outpatient bookings at [client hospital, Mbarara] fell 30% after a rival opened, we weighed price cuts, a radio campaign and WhatsApp booking with SMS reminders. We chose the booking flow because the problem was access, not price, and our team trained the front-desk staff over two weeks. Within ten weeks bookings were 12% above the earlier baseline and missed appointments fell from 22% to 9%, on the hospital's own records (reference and permission on file)."
