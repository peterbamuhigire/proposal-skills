# Weighted Strategic-Factor Analysis and the Strategic Audit

Parent skill: [Business Analysis Tools](../SKILL.md). Extends the SWOT and PESTLE sections of [strategic analysis frameworks](strategic-analysis-frameworks.md).

**When to read:** when a diagnostic, strategy, turnaround, due-diligence or growth assignment must turn a situation scan into a small number of weighted, evidenced strategic factors, or when a proposal offers a strategic audit as a paid diagnostic.

Sources (named frameworks applied in this engine's own procedure and order; synthesised with the 1–4 rating convention associated with Fred David's strategy texts and with general benchmarking practice): external, internal and strategic factor analysis summaries, the industry matrix, the strategic audit and its worksheet, and objective-writing tests from Wheelen, T. L., Hunger, J. D., Hoffman, A. N. and Bamford, C. E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson; comparative SWOT scoring from Johnson, G. et al. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson. Dated statistics excluded.

## 1. Why weight factors

A free-form SWOT is opinion, treats every item as equal, ignores time, and has no validated scoring. Weighted summaries limit the number of factors, weight and rate them, record the reasons, and add a time horizon, so a board can see which factors the strategy answers.

**Scale convention:** this method rates response 1 (poor) to 5 (outstanding), average 3.0. Another common convention rates 1 to 4 with average 2.5. Pick one per deliverable and state it.

## 2. Weighted-factor procedure (EFAS, IFAS, SFAS)

The three named summaries (external, internal and strategic factor analysis summaries, attributed above) share one scoring mechanic. Run them as a single procedure:

**Step 1. Shortlist.** From the external scan (PESTLE, natural environment, competitive forces, scenarios) keep the eight to ten opportunities and threats that most affect the client's position. From the internal review keep eight to ten strengths and weaknesses. A claimed strength stays only if it is valuable, rare, costly to imitate and the organisation is set up to use it; compare each internal factor with the client's own history, named competitors and the sector.

**Step 2. Weight.** Within each list, rank the factors first, fix the most and least important, then assign weights between 0.0 and 1.0 that sum to 1.00 by likely impact on the client's position.

**Step 3. Rate the response.** Score how well the client currently responds to each factor (1 poor to 5 outstanding). Multiply weight by rating and total the list. State the convention on the exhibit.

**Step 4. Justify.** Every row carries a comment: why the factor is on the list, and the evidence behind its weight and rating. Without the comment the row is opinion.

**Step 5. Sanity-check.** Compare each total with the client's actual profitability and market share; investigate any mismatch before continuing.

**Step 6. Combine into strategic factors (SFAS).** In a management review, take the heaviest external and internal factors into one list of fewer than ten, tag each S, W, O or T (dual tags allowed), re-weight to 1.00, carry the ratings across, and add a time-horizon column (under one year, one to three years, over three years). No factor may appear here that was not analysed earlier.

**Step 7. Use the result.** Propose the revised mission and objectives from the strategic factors, and build any options matrix from them alone. Build any strengths-opportunities / weaknesses-threats options grid from these factors only, then test options with the [strategic options evaluation](../../consulting-frameworks/references/strategic-options-evaluation.md).

**Capacity heuristic:** strategy is opportunity divided by capacity (Wheelen and Hunger). An opportunity the organisation cannot yet exploit is not a strategic opportunity; decide whether to build the strength or fix the weakness first.

## 3. Industry matrix and comparative SWOT

- **Industry matrix:** 8 to 10 key success factors, weighted to 1.0, each competitor rated 1 to 5 on its response; totals checked against actual share and profitability. Harmonise the scale with the competitive-position rating already used in the engagement.
- **Comparative SWOT scoring:** score each strength and weakness against each opportunity and threat from +5 to −5, total by row and column, then repeat for two rival groups. The comparison reveals exposure a single-organisation SWOT hides.
- Reject SWOT lists with more than about six items per box, parity "strengths" (things every competitor has), or opportunities with no capacity to exploit them.

## 4. The strategic audit as a diagnostic engagement

The strategic audit (attributed above) is a management-audit checklist. For a client diagnostic, run it as four workstreams in this order:

| Workstream | What it establishes | Main output |
|---|---|---|
| Where the organisation stands | Recent performance; the mission, objectives, strategies and policies as stated and as revealed by behaviour; governance (board or owner family, lenders and investors, succession) | Current-position summary and governance notes |
| What surrounds it | Natural, societal and competitive environment, key actors and forces | External weighted summary |
| What it can do | Structure, culture and resources by function; distinctive capabilities; activities that could be outsourced | Internal weighted summary |
| What it should do next | Strategic factors; options (fine-tuning versus new directions); recommendation; programmes, budgets, owners and timetable; feedback, measures and rewards | Strategic-factor summary, options comparison, implementation and control plan |

**Function review grid (engine format).** For each function (marketing, finance, operations, people, technology, research and development) fill one row:

| Column | Record |
|---|---|
| Direction | The function's current objectives and programmes, and whether they are written down or only visible in its budget and behaviour |
| Fit | Whether they match the mission and the external environment, including regional or country differences |
| Results | Performance, trend, and effect on past and expected results, against comparable organisations |
| Advantage | Whether the function creates advantage, and whether it uses sound, current methods |
| Decisions | Whether its information supports the decisions now pending |
| Sustainability | Environmental and social exposure of the function |
| Leadership | The function head's part in setting strategy |

East African additions to the external workstream: mobile-money penetration and regulation, EAC and AfCFTA trade rules, tax changes (URA, KRA), power reliability, exchange-rate movements; all volatile and to be verified at the time of the assignment.

**Commercial form:** a fixed-fee strategic audit or growth diagnostic is a sound entry offer; the four workstreams become the report's contents, and a worksheet with favourable factors, unfavourable factors and comments per workstream becomes the working-session canvas.

## 5. Objectives, mission and control tests

- **Objective:** starts with "To" and an action verb; has a metric and baseline; a date; is consistent with the mission; has an owner. Anything missing makes it a goal, not an objective.
- **Mission:** short, understandable, directive, says what the organisation does **and does not** do, allows measurable progress.
- **Control:** five to eight steering measures; tolerance bands with exceptions reported; for each measure ask what people could do to hit the number without achieving the goal.
- **Control line:** "[Strategic factor] → [action] → implemented by [role], reviewed by [role], [monthly / quarterly], judged by [measure and tolerance]."

## 6. Proposal wording

"Our diagnostic runs as four workstreams: where the organisation stands today, what surrounds it, what it can do, and what it should do next, ending in an implementation and control plan. Instead of an unweighted SWOT list, we reduce the evidence to fewer than ten strategic factors, each weighted, rated, time-bound and justified, so the board can see exactly which factors the strategy answers."
