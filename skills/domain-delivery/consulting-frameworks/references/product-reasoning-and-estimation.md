# Product and Service Reasoning, Estimation and Pricing Logic

Parent skill: [Consulting Frameworks](../SKILL.md).

**When to read:** when a methodology or recommendation must size demand, judge whether a product or service fits a market, build a recommendation about a product, service or feature, set a price, or choose success measures. It strengthens methodology sections for digital products, service redesign, feasibility studies and new-market entry.

**Sources:** this reference follows the engine's consulting workflow (size → judge fit → build the recommendation → price → measure → critique). Individual techniques draw on structured product-reasoning practice, including Lewis Lin's CIRCLES method and related interview techniques in Lin, L. C. (2013) *Decode and Conquer*, 2nd edn, Impact Interview, and on general consulting practice. Trademarked method names are cited, not reproduced as templates, and all product facts and figures in that book are dated and excluded.

## 1. Size the opportunity

Use two routes and reconcile them:

- **From the whole down:** start from a total (population, households, firms, total spend) and narrow by filters (who needs it, who can pay, who buys this year, share choosing this provider).
- **From one unit up:** start from something observed (customers per hour × spend × hours; users × sessions × actions) and multiply by the number of units.

**Procedure:** fix scope (geography, period, unit) → state the chain of drivers before calculating → state each assumption and calculate as you go, visibly → use ranges and round numbers → allow for peaks, segments and substitutes → check against a known reference and explain the difference → report the answer with its most sensitive assumption.

**Reusable driver chains:** outlet revenue (customers at peak × spend × peak hours, grossed up for off-peak); load (users × sessions × operations ÷ time); replacement demand (population × ownership × replacement rate × category share × provider share); capacity (arrival rate at peak against service capacity); new-line demand (new households or firms per year × share needing it × our share × unit contribution).

Reconcile with the business-plan engine's market-sizing method when a plan is involved, and cite dated sources for every input.

## 2. Judge the fit before recommending

**Market and service fit checklist**

- Market: size, growth, margins, direction of customer preference and regulation.
- Competition: how many, their resources, their distinctive strengths.
- Our fit: expertise, scale, access to distribution, supplier relationships, consistency with the brand promise.
- Hidden operations: installation, bulky logistics, after-sales support (selling a product versus selling a solution).
- Biggest risk: list candidates and justify which one matters most.

**Threats to the client's business model:** start from how the client makes money and the few drivers that matter; test how a competitor, acquisition, complementary product or trend affects each driver. For each threat record demand-side effects and supply-side effects. List the routes to market the client depends on but others control (app stores, marketplaces, mobile-network and mobile-money operators, aggregators). For a disruptor, find its core promise and the weak link it attacks, then compare partnering, building and a low-technology response.

## 3. Build the recommendation

This engine's five-stage recommendation build, informed by structured product-reasoning methods such as CIRCLES:

| Stage | What to do | Output |
|---|---|---|
| **Frame** | Establish what the product or service is, who it serves, why they need it and how it works; confirm the goal (revenue, adoption, retention, cost) and constraints; where the client cannot answer, state an assumption and invite correction | One-paragraph problem frame |
| **Focus** | List possible customer segments and choose one; describe its behaviour and goals; write its needs as user stories ("As a [role], I want [goal] so that [benefit]"); choose the one need to solve first using the prioritisation matrix below | Chosen segment and priority need |
| **Generate** | Produce at least three genuinely different solutions (the second or third is often best) using the idea aids below | Option list |
| **Judge** | Compare options on the same criteria; critique our own favourite openly | Trade-off table |
| **Commit** | State in under thirty seconds what we recommend, why it helps the user and the client, and why it beats the alternatives | Recommendation statement |

Use the stages as a thinking structure and as the spine of a methodology section, not as a script to recite.

**Idea aids**

- **Reverse the situation:** patients cannot come in the evening → the nurse visits the office park at lunchtime.
- **Mix attributes:** list attributes in columns (payment: cash / mobile money / card; booking: in person / WhatsApp / app; timing: on demand / subscription) and combine unusual values.
- **Ask "why?":** challenge a feature everyone takes for granted.

**Prioritisation matrix:** rows are needs, features or initiatives; columns are the chosen criteria graded high, medium or low (value, user satisfaction, ease, whether good alternatives already exist); add an overall priority. Transparent criteria make the ranking open to challenge, which is the point. Value per unit of effort is a quick alternative.

**Illustration (fictional):** raising deposits in a SACCO app. Frame: the goal is deposit frequency. Focus: of boda riders, market vendors and salaried teachers, choose market vendors; story: "As a vendor, I want to save small amounts daily so that I can restock without borrowing." Generate: a USSD daily sweep; WhatsApp reminders with a mobile-money link; agent-collected savings with SMS receipts. Judge and commit: recommend the sweep plus reminders, with the trade-offs recorded.

## 4. Price it

Triangulate three points:

1. **Ceiling:** the customer's best alternative (hiring in-house, a freelancer, doing nothing, a competitor plus add-ons).
2. **Comparables:** prices of similar offers.
3. **Floor:** delivery cost plus the minimum acceptable margin.

Recommend a specific price with its strategic reason and propose a test. Surveys overstate willingness to pay; discount them. For a price change, show the net effect: revenue lost on existing customers + revenue from new customers − extra cost of serving them + extra margin from their other purchases.

## 5. Measure it

State the goal first, then choose measures for each funnel stage the change can affect, dropping stages it cannot affect. For two-sided services, measure both sides. When a test trades one measure against another, decide by the client's declared priority for the period. Explain statistical significance plainly: "how surprising this result would be if the change had no real effect".

## 6. Critique an existing product or site

Declare three criteria before judging (for example useful, easy to understand, honest about charges); walk through how the product actually works; give strengths and weaknesses per criterion with evidence and a comparison; then conclude. Objective standards such as accessibility and performance are judged against their own specifications, not opinion.
