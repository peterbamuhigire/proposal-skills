# Strategic Options Evaluation (Suitability, Acceptability, Feasibility)

Parent skill: [Consulting Frameworks](../SKILL.md). Extends section 11 "Structured Choice" in [strategy frameworks](strategy-frameworks.md).

**When to read:** when a methodology must show how options will be generated and judged (strategy, turnaround, investment, market entry, organisational choices), or when a proposal's own "options considered" section needs a defensible basis.

Sources (methods only, paraphrased): the suitability-acceptability-feasibility system and its screening techniques from Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy: Text and Cases*, 11th edn, Pearson; four criteria for alternatives, strategies to avoid and programmed conflict from Wheelen, T. L., Hunger, J. D., Hoffman, A. N. and Bamford, C. E. (2018) *Concepts in Strategic Management and Business Policy*, 15th edn, Pearson; the strategy diamond (Hambrick and Fredrickson) as summarised there; decision-memo structure from Kelley, L. D. and Sheehan, K. B. (c. 2021) *Advertising Management in a Digital Environment*, Routledge. Dated statistics in these books are excluded.

## 1. Generate real options

1. Generate at least three options from the diagnosis, for example one growth, one stability or consolidation, and one focus or retrenchment option, plus "do nothing" as the baseline.
2. Test every option against four criteria:
   - **Mutually exclusive:** choosing one rules out the others.
   - **Likely to succeed:** feasible, with a reasonable chance of working.
   - **Complete:** addresses every key strategic issue identified.
   - **Internally consistent:** fits the organisation's other goals and policies.
3. Check options against five strategies to avoid: follow the leader; hunting for another single blockbuster; a costly share war; trying to do everything; throwing good money after bad.

## 2. Suitability: does it address the situation?

| Screen | Procedure | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Ranking matrix | Options as rows; key strategic factors from the diagnosis as columns; mark ✓ / ✗ / ?; optional weights; net score; grade A/B/C | Ranked shortlist; the debate behind the scores matters more than the numbers |
| Scenario screen | Test each option against two or four named scenarios | Options that hold up across scenarios, with contingency plans |
| Capability screen | Does the option rest on capabilities that are valuable, rare, hard to imitate and organised to exploit? | Options that use or build real advantage |
| Decision tree | Apply criteria in sequence (growth? low investment? diversification?) | Quick elimination; binary, so use as a first pass only |
| Life-cycle and position matrix | Competitive position (strong, middling, weak) × industry stage (emerging, growing, mature, declining) | Generic direction: e.g. weak in a mature industry → seek alliance, buyer or exit; middling in an early industry → strengthen through alliance or find a niche |

## 3. Acceptability: the three Rs

- **Return:** return on capital employed, payback, discounted cash flow and break-even discount rate; for staged investments, the value of follow-on options; for public or social projects, cost-benefit analysis including externalities. Route formulas and assumptions to the finance engine.
- **Risk:** sensitivity analysis on each key assumption (demand, price, cost, capacity, exchange rate); financial risk (gearing, liquidity: fast growers can run out of cash paying suppliers before customers pay); break-even expressed as the market share required (fixed costs ÷ unit contribution ÷ market size) and compared across options.
- **Reactions:** owners (dilution, control), lenders (security, interest cover), regulators, employees, community, customers and channel partners.

## 4. Feasibility

- **Finance:** timing of funding needs; source matched to the business life-cycle stage (start-up: owner funds and risk equity; growth: growth equity and longer-term debt; maturity: debt and retained earnings; decline: secured debt and retained earnings).
- **People and skills:** capacity, recruitment, training, rewards, relationships.
- **Resource integration:** suppliers, partners and systems that must work together; the plan fails at its weakest link.

## 5. Four qualifications to state in the report

Criteria can conflict and still need judgement; the chosen option must be consistent as a package (competitive approach, direction and method); implementation may reveal new feasibility facts, so stage commitments where possible; real decision processes are not always systematic, so record the reasoning.

## 6. Challenge before recommending

- **Devil's advocate:** a named reviewer presents the case against the preferred option.
- **Dialectical enquiry:** two options built on opposite assumptions are debated to reach a synthesis.
- **Steel-man:** state the strongest case against the recommendation and why it still stands.
- **Outside view:** compare with the track record of similar initiatives, not only the inside plan.

## 7. Recommendation formats

**Strategy diamond (one screen):** arenas (where we will be active), vehicles (organic growth, alliance, acquisition), differentiators (how we will win), staging (speed and sequence), economic logic (how returns are earned).

**Decision memo (one to two pages):** purpose (the decision and why now) → three to five key facts → about three markedly different options, one of which is the status quo → recommendation with about three reasons → action plan (money, people, dates) → contingency ("if X happens, we do Y"). Decide at reasonable confidence; do not recommend more research to avoid deciding.

**Alternative line:** "Alternative [A], [growth / stability / focus]: [one line]. Advantages: [two or three]. Disadvantages: [two or three]. Most-likely five-year result: [figure, source]; downside: [figure]."

**Recommendation line:** "We recommend [option] because it resolves [named strategic factors], fits the organisation's capacity and culture, and puts [amount] at risk for [months]. It requires [policy or structural changes]."

## 8. Proposal wording (methodology)

"Our options phase will generate at least three mutually exclusive options, including the current course. Each is screened for suitability against the diagnosis, for acceptability on return, risk and stakeholder reaction, and for feasibility on funding, people and partners. The board receives a ranked comparison, the options rejected and why, and a recommendation that has been argued against by a named reviewer."

Illustration (fictional, figures to verify): for a 40-room Kampala hotel, options might be a conference wing, a rooftop restaurant open to the public, serviced apartments, a partnership with a regional tour operator, or cost reduction only. A ranking matrix against demand for meetings, a tourism recovery trend, mid-range room oversupply, location strength and weak food-and-beverage skills might favour the restaurant and the partnership, subject to break-even occupancy and sensitivity to room rate and exchange rate.
