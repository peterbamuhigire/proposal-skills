# Strategy Workshops, Hypothesis Testing and Consulting Craft

Parent skill: [Consulting Frameworks](../SKILL.md). Extends section 4 "Hypothesis-Driven Analysis" in [problem structuring](problem-structuring.md).

**When to read:** when a methodology proposes workshops, away-days, co-staffed teams, hypothesis-led analysis, a business case or a strategic plan as a deliverable; and when proposal language must show how the work will move from analysis to action.

**Sources:** organised by this engine's strategy-engagement lifecycle (set up → diagnose → decide together → carry into action). It synthesises general consulting practice (hypothesis-led problem solving, issue trees; see [problem structuring](problem-structuring.md)), decision-science remedies for bias popularised by Kahneman, and strategy-as-practice research as presented in Johnson, G., Whittington, R., Scholes, K., Angwin, D. and Regnér, P. (2017) *Exploring Strategy*, 11th edn, Pearson. Wording and structure are this engine's own.

## 1. Set up the engagement

### 1.1 Decide who takes part

Match participation to two questions: how urgent is the decision, and how far does it break with the past?

| | Continuity with the past | Break with the past |
|---|---|---|
| **Not urgent** | Keep an open, ongoing conversation | Invite broad participation: workshops, staff conferences, structured input |
| **Urgent** | Involve a limited group | Work with a small project team |

Middle managers bring information, interpretation and practical implementation ideas; involve them wherever local adaptation matters.

### 1.2 Be honest about what the analysis is for

Analysis can inform a decision, build buy-in, reassure stakeholders, position an argument politically or, badly, delay a decision. Name the real purpose in the inception report and invest in technical depth only as far as that purpose needs. Where buy-in is the goal, involvement matters more than precision.

### 1.3 Staff it jointly

Mixed client and consultant teams keep knowledge inside the client and make implementation easier. Offer this as good practice: "Each workstream pairs one of our consultants with one of your managers." Informal settings (a working meal with leadership, an informal session with middle managers) help test findings and identify the people who will implement; include them in the governance plan where appropriate and lawful.

### 1.4 Govern it as a project

Write a clear brief that guards against scope creep; secure a committed sponsor; set milestones and reviews; mix skills; respect people's day jobs; and, where several initiatives run at once, watch the whole portfolio to avoid initiative fatigue.

## 2. Diagnose with hypotheses

Run analysis from a small number of testable ideas rather than from data collection for its own sake:

1. Write a **descriptive** hypothesis about how the situation works (for example "scale drives profitability in this market").
2. Look first for the evidence that could disprove it, using quick and rough tests.
3. If it survives, write **prescriptive** hypotheses about what to do (for example "reach scale by acquisition" or "reach scale by alliance").
4. Test those in the same way, dropping weak ones early.

Unlike scientific testing, business testing is time-boxed: it prioritises promising hypotheses and seeks enough support to decide. Client time then goes into the options that matter.

Proposal wording: "We will work hypothesis-first. Our starting hypothesis is [hypothesis]. In weeks 1 to 2 we test it against [data] and drop weak alternatives early."

## 3. Decide together: workshop design

**Purpose first.** Workshops succeed when their purpose is clear. Decide which of two kinds you are running:

| Kind | Design choices |
|---|---|
| **Challenge workshop** (question the strategy) | Challenging tools, a specialist facilitator, a visibly supportive sponsor, flattened hierarchy (off-site venue, structured exercises) |
| **Review workshop** (check progress) | Operational agenda, normal roles, decisions on actions and resources |

**Build bridges back to action:** every workshop ends with named owners, dates and agreed actions; set up project groups for follow-through; run a sequence of workshops (challenge → choice → operational commitment) rather than one event; make senior commitment visible afterwards.

**Avoid the two classic failures:** routine workshops that produce nothing new, and radical workshops that lose touch with what people can actually do; and never let the facilitator be seen to impose an answer.

**Deliverable wording:** "A series of three workshops moves from challenge (facilitated, off-site) to choice to operational commitments; each ends with named owners, dates and a one-page record within two working days."

Room, agenda and minutes discipline: see [orals preparation and room readiness](../../../strategy-positioning/tender-orals-and-proposal-presentation/references/orals-preparation-and-room-readiness.md) section 6.

### Keep decisions sound

| Risk to the decision | Counter-move |
|---|---|
| Looking only for confirming evidence | Always table alternatives and seek disconfirming data |
| Over-reliance on the first number or method | Use a second analytical method |
| Seduction by a vivid analogy or star example | Seek other analogies and list the differences |
| A champion's enthusiasm carrying the room | Hear team members individually; do not let the champion present alone |
| Over-optimistic forecasts | Compare with the track record of similar projects (outside view) |

Run disagreement with rules: an agreed process, a referee, criteria each side could win on, a clear decision rule, and dignity for the side that loses.

## 4. Carry decisions into action

### 4.1 Sell the issue to executives

Keep it brief, link it to critical goals and measures, and attach a proposed solution; issues that arrive with solutions get attention. Use formal and informal channels; build a coalition where needed (which also tests whether the idea holds up); avoid moments of short-term crisis or leadership change.

### 4.2 Business case checklist

- Focus on a few strategic needs, not a departmental wish-list.
- Key data: financial returns, qualitative evidence, short cases, competitor moves, with a note on how rigorous the research was.
- A clear rationale.
- Solutions and actions with owners, barriers, downside scenarios and feasibility.
- Progress measures and review arrangements.
- Use the client's own template where it exists.

### 4.3 Strategic plan checklist

- Mission, goals and objectives consistent with the organisation's earlier statements.
- Environmental analysis that states its implications.
- Capability analysis with a clear statement of advantage.
- The proposed strategy, with the options rejected and why.
- Resources: income, cash flow, balance sheet, people and a realistic timetable.
- Key changes to structure, systems and culture, and how they will be managed.
