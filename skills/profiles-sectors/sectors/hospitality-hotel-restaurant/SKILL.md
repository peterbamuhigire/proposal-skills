Acknowledgement: Shared by Peter Bamuhigire, techguypeter.com, +256 784 464178.

## Use When

- A proposal concerns accommodation, dining, events, catering or hospitality software.

## Do Not Use When

- The work is generic strategy with no hospitality operating model; use the core proposal route.

## Required Inputs

| Artefact | Source | Required? | If absent |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Format, location, capacity, guests, buyer decision and evaluation criteria | Client/tender pack | Yes | Stop and issue a qualified evidence request. |
| Operating, financial, technical and proof evidence | Client/authorised records | Conditional | Mark affected claims `NOT_ASSESSED`. |

## Workflow

1. Classify the hospitality format and buyer decision.
2. Map guest promise to operations, evidence, risks, acceptance and price.
3. Route finance, requirements, website, social and engineering dependencies.
4. Reconcile scope, work plan, team, schedule, assumptions and commercial terms.
5. Run red-team, anti-slop and release checks; stop on unsupported material claims and recover by narrowing scope or requesting evidence.

## Capability and Permission Boundaries

Read/search and authorised drafting are required. Publication, pricing commitment,
client communication and production changes require separate approval.

## Degraded Mode

Return the narrowest qualified proposal outline and an evidence-request list when
brief, proof, finance, currentness or reviewer inputs are missing.

## Decision Rules

| Condition | Action | Risk avoided |
|---|---|---|
| Core hospitality evidence is available | Build an outcome-to-operation proposal | Generic promises |
| A material benchmark or claim is missing | Qualify or withhold it | Fabricated precision |
| Money, tax, inventory or payment is in scope | Route to finance doctrine | Uncontrolled financial commitments |

## Quality Standards

Specific, evaluator-readable, evidence-backed, operationally achievable,
finance-reconciled, risk-aware and handoff-ready.

## Required inputs

- Property or outlet format, location, ownership/management model, capacity,
  service periods, and target guests.
- The buyer's ToR or decision, procurement rules, evaluation criteria, budget
  boundary, delivery dates, and approval authority.
- Verified operating facts: rooms, seats, outlets, event spaces, menus,
  systems, licences, staffing, suppliers, and available proof.
- Financial assumptions from the finance engine: revenue centres, cost centres,
  capex/opex, working capital, taxes, payment flows, and reconciliation owner.

If a material input is missing, label it `NOT_ASSESSED` or make a bounded,
explicit assumption. Never invent occupancy, ADR, covers, margins, reviews,
certifications, client logos, or operating results.

## Hospitality proposal spine

1. **Decision and fit:** define the property/outlet problem, desired outcome,
   guests, operating constraints, and why this response is credible.
2. **Experience and operating model:** connect guest promise to front office,
   housekeeping, kitchen, service, events, maintenance, safety, and management
   ownership. Show hand-offs and failure recovery, not only activities.
3. **Commercial logic:** separate room nights, F&B, events/banquets, ancillary
   services, channel costs, direct demand, and partner commissions. Reconcile
   the financial envelope with the finance doctrine.
4. **Delivery method:** use a staged discovery, design, configuration/build,
   migration, training, pilot, cutover, hypercare, and review plan. Give one
   accountable delivery owner and named client owners.
5. **Evidence and acceptance:** map each promise to proof, an acceptance oracle,
   a test or inspection, and an owner. Mark live-property, browser/device,
   integration, and UAT evidence separately.
6. **Risk and continuity:** cover seasonality, staffing, food safety, guest
   privacy, payments, tax/fiscal obligations, supplier failure, outages,
   double-booking/over-selling, event safety, and recovery.

## Hospitality-specific proof blocks

Include only blocks supported by the brief or evidence pack:

- room/reservation availability and no-double-booking controls;
- arrival, stay, folio, room-charge, checkout, refunds, and night-audit flow;
- housekeeping readiness, maintenance/out-of-order, and service requests;
- restaurant order-to-cash, KOT/KDS, recipe/BOM, waste, stock, and day close;
- event enquiry-to-contract-to-function-sheet-to-debrief controls;
- accounting, payment, tax, audit, privacy, access, and reconciliation design;
- training, adoption, support, operating runbook, and measurable handover.

## Decision rules

- Sell an outcome and controlled operating change, not a feature catalogue.
- Treat hotel and restaurant as connected but separately reconciled revenue
  centres; do not hide cross-posted charges or shared inventory.
- Prefer a small, auditable first release over speculative AI pricing, robots,
  metaverse, biometric identification, or a broad channel-manager promise.
- Explain deferred scope and the evidence required to unlock it.
- Separate technical and financial envelopes where the solicitation requires it.
- For websites and AI/search claims, use verified, entity-rich, truthful content;
  never promise ranking, AI citation, training-data inclusion, or review volume.

## Acceptance checklist

- [ ] Every deliverable requirement has a response, evidence, owner, and oracle.
- [ ] Methodology includes normal, failure, concurrency, approval, and recovery
      paths for the relevant hotel/restaurant workflows.
- [ ] Price and schedule reflect integrations, migration, training, support,
      testing, and client dependencies.
- [ ] Claims are source-backed or explicitly labelled assumption/inference.
- [ ] Risks include guest safety, food safety, privacy, cash/payment, tax,
      seasonality, staffing, and operational continuity.
- [ ] Final red-team checks evaluator compliance, achievability, anti-slop,
      commercial arithmetic, and unsupported hospitality claims.

## Anti-patterns

- Generic “luxury hospitality” prose without a defined guest or buyer outcome. Fix: replace it with a scored, evidence-backed value proposition.
- Unsupported occupancy, ADR, covers, margin, certification or review claims. Fix: cite, qualify or remove them.
- Feature catalogue without operating ownership. Fix: map it to a workflow, owner, dependency, test and handover.
- Treating a demo screen as production proof. Fix: require integration, failure, security, reconciliation and UAT evidence.
- Selling AI, channel or payment capability before controls are ready. Fix: phase it behind explicit release gates.

## Worked example

For a hotel PMS proposal, lead with fewer booking/folio errors and faster room
readiness, then show the reservation, check-in, housekeeping, night-audit and
reconciliation method, evidence to be produced, client dependencies, training,
support and price drivers. Keep OTA synchronisation as a gated option unless
the provider contract and core inventory evidence are available.
